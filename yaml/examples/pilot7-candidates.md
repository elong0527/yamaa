# Candidate fixtures from RConsortium pilot 7 synthetic data

Source: <https://github.com/RConsortium/submissions-pilot7-synthetic-data>
(surveyed at commit on 2026-08-27).

This document proposes fixtures to add under `yaml/examples/`. It does not
change any normative rule. Gap identifiers (`A1`, `C7`, ...) refer to the
taxonomy in [`README.md`](README.md); new gaps proposed here are lettered `H`
onward.

## 1. What the source actually contains

Two live studies, each a single ODM 2.0 XML snapshot with full clinical data:

| study | file | subjects | forms | items | design |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| KEYNOTE-189 (`kn189`) | `kn189/data/odm/kn189_odm.tar.gz` | 616 | 22 | 168 | 1L metastatic non-squamous NSCLC, pembrolizumab + pemetrexed + platinum vs placebo + chemo; 40 study events (Screening, C1D1..C32D1, EOT, Follow-up, Survival Follow-up) |
| KEYNOTE-564 (`kn564`) | `kn564/data/odm/kn564_odm.tar.gz` | 994 | 21 | 129 | adjuvant renal cell carcinoma, pembrolizumab vs placebo; DFS primary endpoint, 30 study events including 8 imaging follow-ups |

Domains present in `kn189` that the current suite has no fixture for: `EX`
(exposure, 13439 records), `RS` (RECIST response, 3611), `RE` (derived
endpoints, 616), `BM` (PD-L1 biomarker), `DC` (baseline oncology
characteristics), `EG` (ECG), `PS` (ECOG), `QS` (PRO scales), `TT` (subsequent
anti-cancer therapy), `RP` (pregnancy test). `kn564` adds `PE` and `PG` and
carries the DFS/OS censoring variables.

Everything below is drawn from that data. Three properties of the source
constrain how it can be used and should be recorded in any fixture README that
draws on it.

### 1.1 The `RE` form is simulated, not derived

`RE` holds `PFS_DAY`, `PFS_EVENT`, `OS_DAY`, `OS_EVENT`, `BESTRESP`,
`CONFIRMED_RESP`, `DOR_DAY`, `VITAL_STATUS`, `LAST_CONTACT_DTC`. These were
generated by the simulator alongside `RS`, not computed from it. Of the 537
`kn189` subjects with `PFS_EVENT = 1` and at least one `PD` assessment,
**`PFS_DAY` equals the first `PD` study day in 7 cases and differs in 530**
(e.g. subject `...SITE001-0028`: `PFS_DAY = 254`, first `PD` at day 272).

Consequence: `RE` is a statement of *which endpoints a real study derives*, and
of their value domains. It is not a golden output. Any time-to-event fixture
must state its own rule and compute its own expected CSV.

### 1.2 The projection in `examples/*/input/odm.csv` drops the form level

The real ODM nests two `ItemGroupData` levels: `Type="Form"` (`FO.*`) wrapping
`Type="Section"` (`IG.*`). The CATH source that the existing fixtures came from
has both levels too, and its long-form ODM projection kept a `FormOID` column —
but the fixture projection `input/odm.csv` has only `ItemGroupOID`, and every
value in it is the `IG.*` level.

In CATH this was harmless: one `LB` form. In `kn189` four forms — `LB_CHEM`,
`LB_HEM`, `LB_THY`, `LB_UA` — each declare their own `LBDTC` item, and all four
are collected in the same study event (`C1D1` and every cycle thereafter). The
leaf name `LBDTC` is therefore ambiguous within one context, and R002's
`ODM.IT.LB.LBDTC` notation cannot resolve it without the form.

R002 already records that "the exact context keys and zero-match or
multiple-match behavior remain unresolved". This is the first concrete case
that forces the answer.

### 1.3 Declared but never collected

`AE.AEENDTC` is declared with `DataType="text"` and is empty in all 6869 AE
records. `EG.ECGINT` is `Normal` in all 616. `IE.IESTRESC` is `Y` in all 616.
A fixture that lifts these verbatim proves nothing; if used, the fixture must
inject the variation it wants to test and say so.

## 2. Proposed minimal-sufficient fixtures

These extend coverage of the existing vocabulary into structures the suite has
not seen. Each is expected to pass; a failure is a finding.

### M1 `odm-form-scoped-item-resolution`

**Source.** `kn189` `LB_CHEM.LBDTC`, `LB_HEM.LBDTC`, `LB_THY.LBDTC`,
`LB_UA.LBDTC` in one study event.

**What it adds.** The first fixture whose `odm.csv` carries `FormOID`, and the
first where the same item leaf name occurs more than once in one context. It
forces R002 to say whether the ODM context key is
(subject, event, event repeat, **form**, item group, item group repeat) and
what happens on a zero or multiple match.

**Fixture shape.** One subject, one study event, four forms, two items each;
output is one SDTM `LB` row per form with `LBDTC` resolved from the right one.
Add a fifth form reference that matches nothing to fix the zero-match answer.

**Why minimal.** Resolves §1.2 with eight input rows.

### M2 `sdtm-ex-combination-regimen`

**Source.** `kn189` `EX`: a `Repeating="Simple"` section nested in each cycle
event, one occurrence per component.

**What it adds.** The first Interventions-class domain in the suite. Within one
subject `EXDOSU` takes three values — `mg` (Pembrolizumab 200), `mg/m2`
(Pemetrexed 500, Cisplatin 75), `AUC` (Carboplatin 5) — so a single dose column
carries three incommensurable units, which is the opposite of the case
`sdtm-vs-unit-standardization` covers. `Saline placebo` at `EXDOSE = 0` must
survive as a collected zero and not be treated as missing. `EXADJ`
(`DOSE INTERRUPTED`, `WITHDRAWN`) is present on only 286 of 13439 records, a
third instance of structural absence versus explicit missingness.

**Fixture shape.** One subject, three cycles × three components, one cycle
carrying `EXADJ`; `EXSEQ` by `row_number` ordered by date then `EXTRT`.

### M3 `adam-adsl-once-measured-carry-forward`

**Source.** `kn189` `VS`. `HEIGHT` is collected at `SCREENING` only — present
in 616 of 12070 `VS` records. `WEIGHT` is collected at every cycle.

**What it adds.** `baseline_flag` / `baseline_value` are currently exercised on
a per-parameter baseline where every parameter has a baseline record
(`adam-adlb-bds`). Here one variable is measured once for the subject's whole
participation and must reach records that do not carry it, while a second
variable in the same form has a value at every visit. Include a subject with no
screening height so `baseline_value` has to produce missing.

**Why it matters.** Every BSA-dosed oncology study needs this, and it decides
whether `baseline_value` is a general subject-constant carry-forward or only a
per-parameter baseline.

### M4 `adam-adsl-stratification-reconciliation`

**Source.** `kn564`. `METSTAT` (`M0` / `M1 NED`) appears on `MH`, `ECOG0` and
`REGIONUS` on `DM`, and all three again on `RE` — the randomization strata
recorded at entry and again at analysis.

**What it adds.** The first fixture where the verification is the point of the
derivation: derive `STRAT1R`/`STRAT1` pairs and assert agreement with
`predicate`, with one seeded discrepancy that must be reported rather than
silently resolved. Nothing in the suite currently contrasts as-randomized with
as-derived.

## 3. Proposed challenge probes

Each is expected to expose a named gap. The point of the fixture is the record
of what could not be written, per the "schema probe" convention in `README.md`.

### P1 `adam-adrs-confirmed-response` — proposes gap **H**

**Rule.** RECIST 1.1 confirmed best overall response: a `PR` or `CR` counts
only if the *next* assessment, at least 28 days later, is also `PR` or `CR`.
`PD` needs no confirmation.

**Source.** `kn189` `RS` carries `OVRLRESP` (`PR` 1242, `SD` 1216, `NE` 616,
`PD` 537) and a `CONFIRMED` flag; 41 `PR` records are unconfirmed and 1201 are
confirmed, so both paths exist in the data.

**Predicted finding — new gap H: no ordered record-to-record access.** No
registered expression reads a neighbouring row of an ordered series.
`row_number` can order records and `baseline_*` can reach one designated
record, but nothing expresses `lead(OVRLRESP)` or the interval to the next
assessment. The only route is `function`, which moves the rule out of the
language.

Gap H is not a variant of D. D is about reductions over a set of rows; H is
about the sequence those rows form. Its other instances in this data: the
interval between consecutive tumour assessments (PFS censoring needs it), the
cycle-to-cycle dose change implied by `EXADJ`, and duration of response, which
runs from the first responding assessment to the next `PD`.

**Fixture shape.** Five subjects: `PR` then `PR` at +42d (confirmed); `PR` then
`SD` at +42d (not confirmed, best response `SD`); `PR` then `PR` at +14d (too
early); `PR` as the last assessment (no confirmatory scan, `NE`); `PD` first.

### P2 `adam-adtte-progression-free-survival` — gaps **D10**, **D11**, **A3**, proposes gap **I**

**Rule.** Event = earliest of first `PD` and death. Censor = last adequate
tumour assessment. Emit `AVAL` (days), `CNSR`, `EVNTDESC`, and the ADaM
traceability triplet `SRCDOM` / `SRCVAR` / `SRCSEQ` identifying the record that
supplied the date.

**Source.** `kn189` `RS` + `DS` (`DISEASE PROGRESSION` 318, `DEATH` 5,
`ADVERSE EVENT` 143) + `RE.LAST_CONTACT_DTC` + `RE.VITAL_STATUS` (`DEAD` 395,
`ALIVE` 221). Expected output computed by the fixture, per §1.1.

**Predicted findings.**
- *D10.* "Earliest of a `PD` date and a death date from different domains" is a
  row-wise minimum over derived columns. `min` reduces one right-side dataset,
  so this becomes null-guarded `case` branches that grow with each candidate
  date — the same shape `sdtm-dm-reference-dates` already had to use, but here
  the result is the primary endpoint.
- *D11.* The date and *which record produced it* come from two independent
  reductions with nothing tying them to the same right-side record. This is the
  existing gap; **gap I** is the observation that ADaM makes it a submission
  requirement, not a convenience: `SRCDOM`/`SRCVAR`/`SRCSEQ` is a regulator-
  facing contract the schema cannot currently honour.
- *A3.* `AVAL` in months needs division by 30.4375. With `divide` unregistered
  it becomes `multiply` by `0.0328549...`, and the constant no longer names the
  rule it encodes.

**Fixture shape.** Five subjects: `PD` before death; death with no `PD`;
ongoing, censored at last assessment; censored with subsequent therapy started
before `PD`; no post-baseline assessment, censored at day 1.

### P3 `adam-adtte-multi-parameter` — gaps **G23**, **F21**

**Source.** `kn564` `RE`: `DFSCNSR`, `DFSDY`, `DFSEVTYP` (`CENSORED` 625,
`RECURRENCE` 361, `DEATH` 8), `OSCNSR`, `OSDY`, plus `RECURSITE` (`DISTANT`
279, `LOCAL` 82, `NONE` 633).

**What it probes.** Two `PARAMCD` rows per subject from one specification, with
a competing-event classifier. Whether `rows` can emit one row per parameter
without the specification growing per parameter, and whether the two parameters
can share a derived intermediate.

**Predicted finding.** Expressible, but each parameter is a hand-written row
template with its derivations duplicated, and there are no named intermediates
(gap B) to share between them. This is gap G23 landing on the BDS structure
that matters most: the specification grows with the endpoint list rather than
describing it.

### P4 `sdtm-lb-ctcae-grading` — gap **A1**, sharpest form

**Rule.** CTCAE v5 grade from a lab value, where thresholds differ per test and,
for haemoglobin, by sex.

**Source.** `kn189` `LB_HEM` already carries `ANC_GRADE`, `HGB_GRADE`,
`PLT_GRADE` alongside `ANC`, `HGB`, `PLT`, so the target values exist and the
fixture only has to reproduce them.

**Predicted finding.** `cut.breaks` is `list[float]` — a literal. A per-test
threshold table cannot be read from data, so the specification needs one `cut`
per test inside a `case`, and sex-dependent haemoglobin breaks need the branch
cross-product. Expressible; grows as tests × strata. A1 states the constraint
abstractly; this makes it concrete on a derivation that appears in every
oncology safety analysis.

### P5 `sdtm-lb-reference-range-indicator` — gap **C7**

**Source.** `kn189` `LB_CHEM` collects bare numbers — `ALT`, `AST`, `TBIL`,
`CREAT`, `ALB`, `SODIUM`, `POTASSIUM`, `GLUCOSE` — with **no unit and no
reference range anywhere in the ODM**. Units and ranges must come from an
external per-test dictionary, which is exactly the real situation.

**What it probes.** `LBORRESU`, `LBSTNRLO`, `LBSTNRHI` all come from one
dictionary row; `mapping_from` returns one column per call, so the same match is
written three times. `LBNRIND` then compares the result to the two looked-up
bounds — the first `case` predicate in the suite that compares two derived
columns rather than a column to a literal.

**Predicted finding.** Expressible with three repeated lookups. C7 on a
derivation every lab dataset needs.

### P6 `adam-adex-cumulative-dose` — proposes gap **J**

**Rule.** Cumulative dose per component, number of cycles administered, and
relative dose intensity.

**Source.** `kn189` `EX`, 13439 records over 616 subjects.

**Predicted finding — new gap J: the aggregate registry has no `sum`, `count`,
or `mean`.** `schema_expression_aggregate.yaml` registers `min` and `max` and
nothing else. Cumulative dose is not expressible, and neither is a count of
cycles. This is a single missing registration with a wide blast radius: subject
counts, event counts, number of metastatic sites (P7 below), and questionnaire
scale scores all fail on it. Relative dose intensity additionally needs
division by a *derived* denominator, which A3 and P8 cover.

Worth stating separately from D: D says aggregates operate on values rather
than rows, which is a design question. J says three ordinary aggregates were
never registered, which is a one-line fix.

### P7 `adam-adsl-metastatic-site-count` — gaps **A1**, **A2**, **J**

**Source.** `kn189` `DC`: `LIVER_METS`, `BONE_METS`, `CNS_METS`,
`ADRENAL_METS` as `Y`/`N` flags, and `N_MET_SITES` as their count. Six input
columns, one expected column.

**Predicted finding.** `add` takes `{source: variable, addend: float}` and
`subtract` takes `{minuend: variable, subtrahend: variable}`. **There is
therefore no way to add two columns together.** The count is reachable only by
mapping each flag to 0/1, negating three of them with `multiply: -1`, and
chaining three `subtract` calls — ten emitted intermediate columns for one
count — or by a `case` with sixteen branches.

**Why it earns its own fixture despite overlapping A1/A2/B.** It is the
smallest possible input that proves the arithmetic asymmetry, and it states the
consequence in one sentence rather than as a property of operand typing.

### P8 `sdtm-eg-qtcf-fridericia` — gap **A3**, boundary case

**Rule.** `QTcF = QT / RR^(1/3)`, `RR = 60 / HR`.

**Source.** `kn189` `EG`: `HR`, `PR`, `QRS`, `QT`, `QTCF` — the target value is
already present, so the fixture reproduces a known answer.

**Predicted finding.** Not expressible. `sdtm-vs-unit-standardization` showed
that a missing `divide` can be worked around with a reciprocal literal, because
the divisor was a constant. Here the divisor is data and the operation is a cube
root, so no literal-operand workaround exists. Three rows are enough to fix the
boundary: A3 is not only about ergonomics, it excludes a class of derivations
entirely.

### P9 `sdtm-epoch-interval-join` — gap **C9**, generalizes `adam-adsl-crossover-periods`

**Rule.** Assign `EPOCH` to every record by locating its date inside a
subject-specific period table.

**Source.** `kn189` `EX` first and last dose, `DS` disposition, and the
Screening / C1D1..C32D1 / EOT / Follow-up / Survival Follow-up event structure.

**What it probes.** With two boundaries this is expressible — `date_diff`
against joined ADSL dates compared to literals inside a `case`. Drive it instead
from a period table with a variable number of rows per subject and the
expressibility ends: there is no interval join, so the specification needs one
`case` branch per period and grows with the data.

**Why it is worth adding even though C9 is already recorded.** It separates the
expressible two-boundary case from the inexpressible n-period case, which is
what a rule proposal needs in order to state a minimum.

### P10 `adam-adqs-scale-score` — gaps **A3**, **J**, constructed input

**Rule.** EORTC QLQ-C30 scale score: raw mean over the scale's items, linear
transformation to 0–100, and the scale is valid only when at least half its
items are answered.

**Source.** `kn189` `QS` carries already-scored scales (`QLQC30_GHS`,
`QLQC30_PF`, `QLQLC13_DYSP`, `EQ5D_VAS`), not the 30 items. **Item-level input
must be constructed**; only the instrument's semantics come from pilot 7.

**Predicted finding.** Not expressible: needs a mean over items (J), division
and rounding (A3), and a count of non-missing values *across columns* rather
than rows — which is a distinct shape from C7's sum across columns.

**Priority.** Lowest of the ten. P6 and P7 already establish J on real data;
this one adds the across-columns completeness rule and can wait.

## 4. Negative fixtures this data makes easy

`README.md` records that failure behavior is asserted but never demonstrated.
Pilot 7 supplies natural cases:

- **N1 duplicate right-side key.** Two `DM` records for one subject must fail
  R003 requirement 4 (right-side uniqueness on applicable keys) rather than
  silently pick one.
- **N2 unmapped value with no handler.** `RS.OVRLRESP` includes `NE` (616
  records, every screening assessment). A response-mapping dictionary that omits
  `NE` and declares no `unmapped` must fail closed.
- **N3 verification failure.** A `PFS` event date before randomization must be
  reported by `predicate`, not written to the output.

## 5. Suggested order

1. **M1** — it decides an unresolved rule (R002 context keys) and blocks any
   further ODM-sourced fixture.
2. **P6 + P7** — one paragraph each, and together they establish gap J and the
   arithmetic asymmetry with the smallest inputs in the suite.
3. **P1** — establishes gap H, which currently has no name.
4. **P2** — the largest single addition; establishes gap I and re-lands D10/D11
   on a regulator-facing contract.
5. **M2, M3, P4, P5** — domain coverage and concrete instances of A1 and C7.
6. **P8, P9, P3, M4, P10** — boundary fixing and structural questions.
7. **N1–N3** alongside whichever fixture first depends on the behavior.

## 6. What this would change in the gap taxonomy

Three additions to `README.md`:

- **H. No ordered record-to-record access.** Nothing reads a neighbouring row
  of an ordered series. Blocks response confirmation, assessment-interval
  censoring rules, duration of response, and cycle-to-cycle comparison.
- **I. Traceability is not expressible.** A derived value cannot carry the
  identity of the source record it came from, so the ADaM `SRCDOM`/`SRCVAR`/
  `SRCSEQ` contract cannot be met. This is D11 restated as a submission
  requirement rather than a convenience.
- **J. `sum`, `count`, and `mean` are unregistered.** Distinct from the rest of
  D: not a design question, a missing registration.

And one sharpening of A: `add` accepts a literal addend and `subtract` accepts
two variables, so **the schema can subtract two columns but cannot add them**.
